If you want something cool to sip while you have a cough, this pineapple, lemon and ginger drink is easy to blend. It is a drink, not a cough treatment: there is no good evidence that pineapple juice cures a cough or that the amount of bromelain in a glass of juice clears mucus. [NCCIH's bromelain overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/bromelain) describes the limits of the evidence.

![Homemade pineapple juice with pineapple slices](/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Untitled-design-2024-09-19T125531.242-1024x1024.png)

## How to make pineapple juice with ginger and lemon

### Ingredients

- 1 medium ripe pineapple, peeled and chopped
- Juice of 1 lemon
- 1 inch fresh ginger, peeled (use less for a milder drink)
- 1 tablespoon honey, optional
- A pinch of salt, optional
- Cold water or ice, as needed to blend

### Instructions

1. Put the pineapple, ginger and lemon juice in a blender. Add a little cold water if needed and blend until smooth.
2. Strain if you prefer a thinner juice.
3. Stir in the optional honey and salt to taste. Serve chilled.

Pineapple and lemon can feel sharp on an irritated throat. Skip the citrus or dilute the drink if it feels uncomfortable. Honey may help relieve cough symptoms for adults and children **at least 1 year old**, according to the [CDC's advice for chest colds](https://www.cdc.gov/acute-bronchitis/about/index.html). **Never give honey to a child under 12 months** because of the risk of infant botulism. This recipe is not a substitute for medical care if a cough is severe, persistent or accompanied by breathing trouble.

### Storage and variations

Refrigerate promptly in a clean covered container and make only what you expect to drink soon. Fresh juice is best served the day it is made. For a milder version, use less ginger and add water or ice. You can omit honey or sweeten to taste; maple syrup changes the flavor but does not have the same evidence for cough relief as honey.
